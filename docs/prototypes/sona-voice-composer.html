Now

  1. 1You speak. Words fill the draft bubble, as today. Below it: ✎ edit and 🗑 clear, as quiet icons, then a small dark ↑ to send (the same send as the composer).
  2. 2Tap ✎ (or the bubble, or the keyboard button). The bar turns into the composer, with the words in it. The mic stays on, and the wave stays up.
  3. 3Keep talking. New words come in grey at the end, then go black once they are read.
  4. 4Tap the text to fix a word. The keyboard comes up and the mic pauses, so speech and typing never edit at the same time.
  5. 5Tap Listening (the mic) to talk again. The keyboard goes down, and new words are added after your edit.
  6. 6Send ↑ (or say “submit”). The edited text is sent.

What changes from today

Today

The keyboard button moves the draft into the composer, but it also turns the mic off and ends voice. To say more, you start over.

This prototype

The composer and voice stay open together. The draft can be edited, and talking adds to it. Typing pauses the mic, and the mic button resumes it.

Left out on purpose

Words go at the end, not where the cursor is. Inserting mid-sentence by voice adds a lot of code for little gain.
