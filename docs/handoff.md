## Accepted product and visual direction

Clinician/hospital B2B iPhone assistant for the healthcare competition: ambient documentation, intelligent workflows, triage support and hospital operations with privacy-focused open-weight models.

- One conversational workspace with automatic capability routing. The current router and responses are fixtures; arbitrary natural-language understanding is a backend task.
- Seven capabilities: briefing/timeline including medical/family/social history; ambient scribe and editable note; clinical possibilities with evidence/gaps; chest X-ray; medication interactions; autonomous synthetic prescribing; follow-up tasks.
- Autonomous prescribing intentionally has no clinician approval gate in the supported synthetic case. It still blocks missing inputs/stale checks, records sources and creates an immutable simulation receipt. Preserve that accepted behavior.
- Monogram-first liquid glass, pale grey canvas, subtle blue wash replacing the supplied Oria pink, charcoal type and selective Arc progress/source effects. Read [design direction](design-direction.md) and [UI specification](ui-specification.md) before changing the design. Supplied PNGs are in `docs/references/`.
- English baseline, bounded Malay/English/Mandarin Manglish demonstration. Current dialogue is scripted; real STT/TTS/S2S and code-switching capability have not been demonstrated.
- Baseten cloud is the intended inference platform, with hybrid deployment later. MedGemma is intended for medical/multimodal work; Gemma 4 is a candidate to evaluate, not a deployed model. Unsloth is intended for potential training. Budget was described as ample. Deployment, privacy/compliance and model capability still need actual evaluation.

For feature details, consult [PRD](prd.md), [storyboard](demo-storyboard.md) and [fixture plan](ui-fixture-plan.md) as needed. Proposed hospital ops demo flows (discharge, guarantee letter, ward view, evidence answers) are ranked in [ops demo flows](research/ops-demo-flows.md).

Findings to act on:
- **Cold start is 9–16 minutes** on the GPU boxes (vLLM start-up and torch.compile dominate); Laya's is 31 s. A request held open through a wake had its connection reset, so the Worker must wake a box, poll `/health`, then call it, and the demo warm-up starts at least 15 minutes early.
- **TTS speed.** Measured 1 Oct at the rated settings: plain VoxCPM took 2.0–2.8× the audio's length (a 45-character sentence, 4 s of audio, took 8–11 s) and 1.3–1.7× compiled (`day2-fast`, `wn4gj11`). Production is now `day2-nano2` (`qe9pll5`, promoted 1 Oct): Nano-vLLM-VoxCPM (CUDA graphs over the whole step) makes a sentence in about 1 s on the box (≈ 0.3× real time) and sends MP3 (49 KB instead of 420 for 4.5 s), so a short sentence reaches Malaysia in about 2.5 s; `day2-fast` and `day1d` are kept for rollback. Box ② now takes about 25 minutes to start (a 17 GB image, two Python environments on 4 CPUs). All four check sentences (en, ms, zh, the 999 line) were heard back word for word by the ASR. 4 `inference_timesteps` (uncompiled) only brought it to ≈ 1.5 and is unrated by ear. Each LM step makes 160 ms of audio and the L4's bandwidth allows about 26 ms per step, so the remaining time is VoxCPM's Python loop, not the GPU. Sona Care's voice mode reads answers in this voice anyway (`care.speak`, `apps/care/features/assistant/sona-voice.ts`): sentence by sentence, each kept in the phone's cache so a replay is instant and gap-free; a first sentence not ready in 8 s is read by the phone's voice, labelled, while the rest is still made for next time. - **The scribe tells any number of people apart.** A part sent to `voice.transcribe` with `voices` comes back as numbered voices: NVIDIA's Nemotron-3-Diarization (100M, up to 8 people, `voice/data/diar_server.py` in a third venv) finds each voice's turns, the ASR writes each turn down on its own (`voice/data/voices.py`), and the answer's `memory` (the model's speaker cache, about 0.4 MB) goes back with the next part, which is what keeps a person's number the same all consultation though the box keeps nothing. The app sends a visit's parts one at a time for that (`features/scribe/live-recorder.tsx`), shows "3 voices so far", and once stopped asks "Who is Voice 3?" with the longest thing that voice said: one tap names it (Clinician, Patient, Family, Nurse, Interpreter, Other, or a typed name), "Same person as" merges a voice heard twice, and correcting one line moves only that line (`features/scribe/live.ts`, `recording-screen.tsx`; prototype in `docs/prototypes/sona-scribe-speakers.html`). Gemma then names each voice once instead of guessing every sentence, and a fact from a relative or nurse says who gave it (`packages/api/src/scribe-model.ts`). A box without the diarizer, or a part where it fails, answers as before: one caption under its time.
- **Measured on 20 synthetic consultations of 2 to 8 people** (`tools/diar-eval`: `build.py` stitches the voiced Manglish turns with room echo, noise and overlaps; `run.py`, `segments.py`, `words.py` score them). Turns given to the right person: 99.1% in 30 s parts with the memory handed on, as good as the whole recording in one pass (99.3%), against 25.7% with no memory. Words wrong: 6.5% when each diarized turn is transcribed alone (7.2% counted by person), against 9.4% for whole parts with nobody's name on them, so no aligner is needed. Eight people is the edge (one recording found six).
- **A real voice can be heard as two.** The user's 15 recorded lines are one person in two registers (clips 08-12 near 120 Hz, the rest near 165 Hz), and the model split them in 3 recordings of 10; neither its own memory nor a voiceprint model could say they were one person, so merging is the doctor's tap, never automatic. Synthetic voices are cleaner than people: a recording of three or four real people in a room is still to be scored.
- **Voiceprint model picked, not wired** (`tools/diar-eval/voiceprint.py`): 3D-Speaker CAM++ zh-en (ONNX, 28 MB, Apache-2.0) tells real strangers apart at 7% equal error on 3 s clips (about 2% with mislabelled clips dropped), threshold 0.47. It is for the live call (is this turn the patient or someone else?), which is designed but waits on the user's choice of where a voiceprint may be held.
- **Sona's voice no longer changes speaker between sentences.** An answer is made a sentence at a time, and with no reference and no seed VoxCPM2 drew a slightly different speaker per call (1 and 3 answers of 18 in two runs). `voice/data/tts_server.py` now speaks every line from the reference clip in the line's language (`voice/data/anchors`, picked by `spoken.py`) and one seed: no answer had an off sentence and no words were lost (`tools/tts-eval/speaker_gen.py`, `speaker_prints.py`, `speaker_heard.py`; measured on a Mac with VoxCPM's own engine, where a seed is `torch.manual_seed`, so the box's engine is still to be measured the same way). One clip for all three languages turned a Malay line into garbled English, and a seed alone misread a dose, so keep both parts. Sona Care's cached sentences are keyed by the new voice version. The user listened on 6 Oct to one three-sentence English answer made both ways (`~/zyt/data/sona-tts/speaker/listen`): the old one "poor", the new one "so much better and consistent, felt like spoken by a same person". Between languages she is close but not quite one woman, which the user also heard: the training voice's own clips are less alike across languages (Mandarin 0.51 to the other two, against 0.60-0.66 within a language, `tools/tts-eval/anchors.py`). Three clips picked to match each other (0.72 between any two; `en_0054`, `ms_0591`, `zh_0565` of the Kore set) moved sentences in different languages only from 0.43 to 0.48 alike and lowered the worst pair inside an answer a little, so the box keeps the three it was proven with; closing that gap is a retraining with a reference clip in every example (about $10 of H100, to ask the user).
- **Known before this work:** `packages/api/src/leaflets.test.ts` "the gates fire first" fails (Gemma is asked twice); it fails the same without these changes.

### Scanned leaflets and photos in the library, checked by staff (7 Oct 2026, on staging)

A scanned PDF (no text layer) used to be refused; now `library.readScan` has MedGemma (box ③) read each page into text, without saving, and the upload screens show every page's picture beside its editable text (web `routes/pro/_app/library.tsx`, phone `features/library/add-source.tsx`) with the words the model was unsure of (its token probabilities, `readingOf`) in amber; "Checked, add to library" calls `library.addScan`, which saves the text as `kind: "scan"` with "checked by staff" in the source. Photos of printed pages (JPEG or PNG, up to 10) go the same way. `library.addPdf` refuses a scan with `reason: "scan"` and the screen hands it to the review. Why the review: on the real test MedGemma read English and Malay pages with 0-0.5% of characters wrong, Chinese with 5-27% (one Chinese safety line was replaced with made-up text), so nothing read is searched or cited until a person has checked it. Measured on staging (7 Oct): a 3-page scanned PDF read in 8 s, two photos in 2 s, saving 1 s. Test material in `~/zyt/data/sona-scan`, the walk-through in `apps/server/test/scan-upload.ts`. Not built: tapping a page to see it larger; HEIC photos from an iPhone (JPEG and PNG only); a stronger reader for Chinese (PaddleOCR-VL is the candidate) if the review catches too much.

**EmbeddingGemma 2 was tested and not adopted** (`tools/embed-eval`): on 56 patient questions in English, Malay, Chinese and Manglish over the demo leaflets it tied the library's EmbeddingGemma-300M when the leaflets stood alone (96% right first) and lost with 1,800 nurse lines as distractors (MRR 0.45 against 0.54, English 0.39 against 0.74), at 1.6x the time per question. Its image and audio embeddings were not needed: scans are read into text instead. Fine-tuning it on what we have (7 Oct, `tools/embed-eval/train.py`, on the Mac, free) made it worse, twice: the only question-to-answer pairs we own are the synthetic consultations' patient turn and doctor reply, and training on them teaches that a question belongs with a spoken clinician line, which is the shape of the distractors (MRR 0.38 with the replies untitled, 0.24 titled with the condition plus mined hard negatives). A fine-tune that helps needs real patient questions paired with the documents that answered them, which the library can log once a hospital uses it; until then the stock 300M model stays.

**The library's embedder runs in Malaysia (7 Oct 2026, on staging):** EmbeddingGemma-300M on its own always-on CPU box, `sona-embed` on SageMaker (`packages/infra/aws/embed`, ml.c6i.large, int8, about $0.12 an hour), beside the other models and off Workers AI; `SAGEMAKER_ENDPOINTS` names it as `embed=sona-embed`, and without that name the Worker uses Workers AI as before. Measured from the Mac: a question embeds in ~130 ms, a short section ~110 ms, a 600-token section ~1 s (the Worker sends 20 sections a call and waits up to 60 s, so a long PDF's upload takes longer than on Workers AI, which ran on GPUs). The staging library was re-indexed on it (`library.reindex`, management only). `test.sh` brings the box up or updates it in place and leaves it running; `DOWN=1` takes it down.

### Cloudflare Agents (1 Oct 2026)

Two Durable Objects on the Agents SDK (`agents` 0.24), bound in `packages/infra/alchemy.run.ts` and live on staging.

- **Check-in agent** (`CheckInAgent`, `apps/server/src/agents/check-in.ts`, binding `CHECK_IN_AGENTS`): one per discharged visit, started by the discharge. The decisions are `decideFollowUp`'s (`packages/domain/src/check-in-agent.ts`). Because the demo clock moves "due" under the alarm, a clock jump, each reply and the hourly cron also call `tick`.
- **Live voice call** (`SonaVoiceAgent`, `apps/server/src/agents/voice.ts`, binding `VOICE`, `withVoice`): Sona Care's hands-free conversation, one agent per patient at `/agents/sona-voice-agent/<user id>?lang=<en|ms|zh>` with the session cookie; the Worker names the agent from the session, never from the app. Our ASR hears each turn behind an energy VAD (`packages/api/src/voice-agent/listen.ts`), Gemma answers anything the patient asks, their own care from their plan, sentence by sentence (`answer.ts`; on 1 Oct the user removed every content limit, so no sentence is held back, and only the 999 line and care-team tasks stay), Sona's voice speaks it as one MP3 per sentence (`voice.ts`), and a symptom the patient reports reaches their care team as a flagged task (`care-team.ts`). The phone side is `apps/care/lib/live-voice/` (react-native-audio-api in the `voiceChat` session, imported lazily so Expo Go still runs). Instead of iOS echo cancellation, an echo gate mutes the mic while Sona is audible and for 800 ms after; cutting in takes speech louder than her echo for 200 ms (`gate.ts`, constants to tune on a device). Call it from a laptop with `bun apps/server/test/voice-call.ts <worker url> <16 kHz wav> --lang ms`. Measured on staging with the user's recorded Manglish line: warm, heard 1.5 s after the patient stopped, first words 2.3 s, first audio 3.5 s, answer finished 9.5 s, care-team task created; the first call after a deploy was cold (heard at 17.7 s, every sentence read by the phone's voice). A sentence Sona's voice was too slow for is read by the phone's voice in its place, and the agent warms the voice and brain boxes when the phone connects (`warmBoxes`): box ② stalls 12–20 s on its first requests after a quiet spell even while its replica is up (Baseten's own timing, GPU idle; cause unknown, and finding it needs a new deployment). After the keep-awake window, opening voice mode therefore wakes sleeping boxes (Baseten starts a box on any request, the health check included). Not yet: Sona Pro's mic does not use the agent, and one answer said "Saya sudah maklumkan pasukan penjagaan anda." twice.
